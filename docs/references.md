# Vibes Fund: References

These are the references and sources behind the design and the plan. A style description is not a reference: each direction is designed from real artifacts of its genre.

## Design references by direction

**A. Mood Ring Capital (1970s two color annual report)**
- 1970s mutual fund annual reports and shareholder letters: financial highlights tables, ruled schedules of investments, the chairman's letter set in columns.
- Swiss style corporate reports of the same era: strict grids, flush left type, big numerals, lots of empty space.
- Two color offset printing: one spot ink over black, with halftone dot screens. This is the source of the "halftone everywhere" rule.
- Original 1975 mood ring packaging and the "what your color means" chart card.

**B. Sentimental Value Partners (old money prospectus)**
- Engraved stock and bond certificates: guilloche borders, vignettes, serial numbers, engraved roman capitals.
- Private bank letterheads and correspondence from the early 1900s.
- Bound ledger books: ruled columns, red margin lines, handwritten style entries set in type.
- Banknote line engraving for portraits: hatching and cross hatching, never flat fills.

**Character illustration (the Chairman's portrait)**
- The classic mid-century magazine gag cartoon: fine pen line, cross hatching for shadow, solid blacks, simplified anatomy. In particular, the 1920s to 1950s New Yorker cartoonists Rea Irvin and Peter Arno.
- Rule: borrow only the drawing language. Never use The New Yorker's own mascot (the top hat gentleman) or its logo or lettering. Those are its brand marks.
- [Rea Irvin (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rea_Irvin)
- [Peter Arno: The Mad, Mad World of The New Yorker's Greatest Cartoonist (The Comics Journal)](https://www.tcj.com/reviews/peter-arno-the-mad-mad-world-of-the-new-yorkers-greatest-cartoonist/)
- How a teacup is actually held, used for the Chairman's hands: thumb and index finger pinch the top of the handle, the middle finger braces underneath, the other fingers tuck in, and the saucer is held at belly height in the other hand.
  - [Beaumont Etiquette: how to hold a teacup](https://www.beaumontetiquette.com/post/how-to-hold-a-teacup-correctly)
  - [Mala Casa: the right way to hold an afternoon tea cup](https://malacasa.com/blogs/journal/how-to-hold-a-teacup)
